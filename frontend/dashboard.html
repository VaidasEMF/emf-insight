Create your first Home Property

Set up your property profile and begin your Home Wellness journey.

Creating a Home Property is the first step in building your property's environmental profile in EMF Insight.

1. Property name

Enter a name that will help you identify the property, for example Family Home.

2. Property information

Complete the main information about the property:

  • Property type
  • Country
  • City / Town
  • Postal / Postcode
  • Street address
  • Apartment / Unit

3. Your account information

Your Home Property setup also uses your account profile information so that the property is associated with your account.

4. Create My Property

When the required information is complete, select Create My Property.

EMF Insight validates the information before creating the Home Property.

5. What happens next?

After the property is created, it becomes part of your Home Wellness account and can be accessed from My Property.

Your property can then be used as the foundation for your Home Wellness assessment and Property Health Record.

Tip You can return to My Property later to open an existing property or create another Home Property.

Understanding Property Insights

Understand how EMF Insight presents your property's assessment results.

Property Insights brings together the information collected during your Home Wellness assessment and presents it in a way that helps you understand your property's environmental profile.

Property assessment

Your property assessment is built from the information, measurements and environmental data collected for the property.

The assessment becomes part of the property's ongoing Property Health Record.

Exposure results

Property Insights presents the available exposure information collected during the assessment, including the relevant measurement results and their location within the property.

Heatmaps and measurement coverage

Where measurements have been collected, visual results such as heatmaps help show how exposure varies across rooms and assessed areas.

Measurement coverage also provides context for understanding how much of the property has actually been assessed.

Sources and recommendations

Property Insights can also present identified EMF sources and recommendations related to the assessment.

Recommendations are intended to help you understand possible next steps based on the assessment information.

Full EMF Insight Report

When available, the complete EMF Insight Report provides a more detailed presentation of the property's assessment and findings.

Important Property Insights reflect the information and measurements available for the property at the time of the assessment. Measurement coverage is therefore important context when interpreting results.

Home Wellness vs Business Survey

Understand the purpose of each EMF Insight workspace.

EMF Insight provides two separate workspaces designed for different assessment contexts: Home Wellness and Business Survey.

Home Wellness

Home Wellness is designed for individual property owners who want to understand the environmental profile of their home and its living areas.

A Home Property forms the foundation of the Home Wellness workflow and is connected to its Property Health Record.

Business Survey

Business Survey is designed for professional assessment workflows involving projects, floors, rooms, zones, measurements, heatmaps and reporting.

It provides a structured workspace for carrying out and documenting professional property assessments.

Choose the appropriate workspace

Use Home Wellness when you are working with your own property as a Home user.

Use Business Survey when carrying out professional assessment work and managing Business Survey projects.

Tip Your workspace determines which workflows, projects and account features are available to you.

Creating a Home Property

Set up your property before beginning your Home Wellness assessment.

A Home Property is the foundation of your Home Wellness experience. It connects your property information with its Property Health Record and future assessments.

Start a new property

From the Home Wellness workspace, choose New Property to begin the property setup.

Enter your property information

Provide the main information used to identify and describe your property:

  • Property name
  • Property type
  • Country
  • City / Town
  • Postal / Postcode
  • Street address
  • Apartment / Unit

Complete your personal information

Your Home Wellness property is associated with your account profile. Make sure your first name, last name and email address are correct.

Create My Property

Once the required information has been completed, select Create My Property.

EMF Insight validates the information and creates the property when the required details are valid.

After your property is created

Your new property becomes available through My Property and is connected to its Property Health Record.

You can then continue with the Home Wellness workflow and build the property's assessment over time.

Tip You can create more than one Home Property and return to your existing properties from My Property.

Your Property Health Record

Understand the ongoing health record associated with your property.

Your Property Health Record is the ongoing record associated with your property. It provides the foundation for keeping information about the property's assessments connected over time.

One property, an ongoing record

A Home Property is not just a single assessment. Its Property Health Record provides a continuing place for the property's assessment information.

Assessments over time

As assessments are performed, they can be associated with the same property record. This allows the property's assessment history to develop over time.

Property information and assessments

The property profile provides the identity and context of the property, while assessments provide information collected during individual assessment activities.

Why this matters

Keeping assessment information connected to the property makes it possible to understand the property's environmental profile as an ongoing record rather than as isolated assessments.

Remember Your Property Health Record belongs to the property and can continue to develop as new assessments are added.

Property Insights

Understand the information and results available for your property.

Property Insights brings together the information available from your property assessment so you can understand what has been measured, where it was measured and what the assessment indicates.

Property overview

The Property Insights view provides an overview of the property's current assessment information and status.

Measurements and coverage

Assessment results can include measurement information and measurement coverage for the assessed areas of the property.

Coverage is important context because results reflect the areas that have actually been assessed.

Exposure information

Where measurement data is available, Property Insights can present the relevant exposure information collected during the assessment.

Sources and recommendations

The assessment may also identify relevant EMF sources and provide recommendations associated with the available assessment information.

Assessment context

Property Insights should be understood in the context of the assessment that produced the available data, including its measurement coverage and the areas assessed.

Important Property Insights are based on the information available from the property's assessment. They should be considered together with the assessment context and measurement coverage.

Full EMF Insight Report

Understand the complete report available for your property assessment.

The Full EMF Insight Report provides a more complete, structured presentation of the information generated during your property assessment.

When is the report available?

The Full EMF Insight Report becomes available when the required assessment information is present for the property and the report has been unlocked.

What does the report bring together?

The report brings together the available assessment information into a single document, making it easier to review and retain the results of the property assessment.

  • Property and assessment context
  • Assessment results
  • Measurement and coverage information
  • Relevant EMF sources
  • Recommendations
  • Supporting visual information

Unlocking the report

The Full EMF Insight Report is a separate one-time purchase associated with the property assessment.

Once unlocked, the complete report can be generated for the property.

Report vs Property Insights

Property Insights provides the interactive assessment information within EMF Insight. The Full EMF Insight Report brings the available assessment information together into a complete document.

Important The report reflects the assessment information available for the property at the time it is generated.

Creating a Business Project

Understand how a Business Survey project is organized.

A Business Project provides the workspace for carrying out and documenting a professional property survey.

Workspace

Business Survey is a separate workspace designed for professional assessment workflows.

Project

A project represents the property or survey engagement you are working on.

Project information provides the foundation for organizing the survey and its associated data.

Session

A project can contain survey sessions. A session represents a particular assessment activity within the project.

Sessions allow survey work to be organized over time rather than treating every assessment as one continuous activity.

Current Floor

Within a survey session, work is performed on the current floor of the property.

Floors provide the spatial structure for rooms, zones and measurements.

Survey workflow

The Business Survey workflow guides you through the main stages of preparing and measuring the property.

  • Plan
  • Scale
  • Rooms
  • Zones
  • Measure
Tip Keep your project structure organized from the beginning. It makes later measurements, results and reporting easier to manage.

Sessions, Floors & Rooms

Understand how the physical structure of a property is organized within a Business Survey.

Business Survey organizes assessment work around the physical structure of the property. Sessions, floors and rooms provide the structure in which survey data is collected and reviewed.

Sessions

A session represents a particular survey activity within a Business Project.

Sessions allow assessment work to be separated into distinct survey activities over time.

Floors

A property can contain multiple floors. The current floor determines the part of the property you are working on during the survey.

Each floor can contain its own rooms, zones and measurement information.

Rooms

Rooms define the individual spaces within a floor. They provide the spatial context for measurements, sources and assessment results.

Why the structure matters

Keeping sessions, floors and rooms organized makes it possible to understand where measurements were collected and how results relate to the property.

Tip Complete the floor and room structure before collecting measurements so that your survey data remains spatially organized.

Zones & Measurements

Understand how survey areas and measurement data are organized.

Zones and measurements provide the structure for collecting and organizing environmental data during a Business Survey.

Zones

Zones define specific areas within the property that can be assessed separately from the overall room structure.

A zone can be used to organize measurements around a particular area or assessment context.

Measurement Profile

The Measurement Profile determines which measurement modalities are collected for the survey.

  • RF
  • Electric
  • Magnetic

The selected profile affects which measurements are collected and contributes to assessment completion and confidence.

Measurement points

Measurement points represent locations where measurements are collected within the room or zone.

Multiple measurement points can be used to build a more complete picture of the assessed area.

Coverage

Measurement coverage indicates how much of the relevant area has been measured.

Coverage provides important context when reviewing survey results and determining whether an area has been sufficiently assessed.

Important Measurement Profile determines what is collected. It does not itself determine the property's risk level.

Heatmaps & Results

Understand how measurement data is visualized and reviewed.

Heatmaps provide a visual representation of measurement data across the assessed areas of a property.

From measurements to a heatmap

Measurements collected at points within rooms and zones provide the underlying data used to generate the visual representation.

Spatial context

Because measurements are associated with locations within the property, the results can be reviewed in relation to rooms, zones and floors.

Measurement coverage

Coverage provides context for interpreting a heatmap. Areas without measurement data should not be interpreted as equivalent to areas that have been measured.

Reviewing results

Heatmaps can be reviewed together with the underlying measurements, sources and other assessment information available for the survey.

Different floors

Where a property contains multiple floors, each floor can have its own spatial assessment information and visual results.

Important A heatmap visualizes available measurement data. It does not replace the underlying measurements or their assessment context.

Business Survey Reports

Understand how survey information is brought together in reports.

Business Survey reports bring together the information collected during an assessment so that the results can be reviewed as a structured assessment.

What a report brings together

Report content can include information from the property structure, rooms, zones, measurements, sources, heatmaps and assessment results.

Assessment context

Results should be interpreted together with the context of the survey, including the areas assessed and the measurement coverage available for those areas.

Before generating a report

  • Check the property and floor structure.
  • Review rooms and zones.
  • Check available measurements.
  • Review measurement coverage.
  • Review sources and results.

Report and underlying survey data

A report is a presentation of the assessment information. The underlying survey data remains the basis for the results and should be retained as part of the assessment record.

Tip Review the survey results before generating the final report so that missing measurements or incomplete areas can be identified first.

Login & Account

Common checks when you have problems accessing or updating your account.

If something is not working correctly with your account, check the following before contacting support.

Cannot sign in

Check that you are using the email address associated with your EMF Insight account and that your password is entered correctly.

Password problems

If you are already signed in, open Account → Security to change your password.

A new password must contain at least 8 characters and must be different from the current password.

Profile changes are not visible

Open Account → Profile and check that the information was saved successfully.

Reopen Profile to verify that the saved information is loaded from your account.

Still having a problem?

If the issue continues, note what you were trying to do, what happened, and any error message displayed by the platform before contacting support.

Tip When reporting an account problem, include the exact step where the problem occurred. This makes the issue easier to investigate.

Property & Project Issues

Common checks when working with Home properties and projects.

If a property or project does not behave as expected, check the property list and the project you are trying to open.

Property is not visible in My Property

Open Account → My Property and check the recent property list.

If you have several properties, select View all properties to display the complete list.

Cannot open a property

Select Open for the property you want to continue working with.

The selected property should open in the Home Wellness workspace.

Creating a new property

Use + Add Property from My Property, or start the Home property creation workflow.

Required property and personal information must be completed before the property can be created.

Property information looks incorrect

Check the property profile information entered during property creation, including the property name, type, country and city or town.

If the problem concerns your personal account information, check Account → Profile separately.

Tip When reporting a property issue, include the property name and the action you were performing when the problem occurred.

Measurements & Results

Common checks when measurements or assessment results do not appear as expected.

Measurement results depend on the areas and points that have been measured. When reviewing results, first check the underlying survey structure and measurement coverage.

Measurements are missing

Check that measurement points have actually been collected for the room or zone you are reviewing.

Also check that you are viewing the correct floor, room or zone.

Coverage is incomplete

Incomplete coverage means that only part of the relevant area has measurement data.

Review the measurement points and complete the relevant areas before treating the results as a complete assessment.

Heatmap does not show the expected result

Check that the relevant area contains measurement data and that you are viewing the correct floor or room.

Remember that a heatmap represents available measurement data and should be interpreted together with coverage.

Results look different between areas

Different rooms or zones can contain different measurement coverage and measurement data.

Review the underlying measurements and spatial context before comparing results.

Important Do not interpret an unmeasured area as equivalent to an area that contains confirmed measurement data.

Reports

Common checks when a report does not generate or does not contain the expected information.

Reports are generated from the assessment information available for the property or business survey.

Report does not generate

Check that the assessment contains the required survey information and that the relevant property, floor and measurement data are available.

Report is missing information

Review the underlying assessment and check that the relevant rooms, zones, measurements and other assessment information have been completed.

Heatmap or measurement information is missing

Check the underlying measurement coverage and confirm that the relevant areas contain measurement data.

Before contacting support

  • Check the property or project you are working with.
  • Check the relevant floor and room.
  • Check measurement coverage.
  • Check whether the underlying results are available.
Tip When reporting a report-generation problem, include the property or project, the report type and the step at which the problem occurred.

Technical Issues

Basic checks for unexpected platform behaviour.

If the platform behaves unexpectedly, a few basic checks can help determine whether the problem is temporary or specific to the current session.

Refresh the page

Refresh the current page and check whether the problem remains.

Check the current workspace

Confirm that you are working in the correct workspace, such as Home Wellness or Business Survey.

Check the current property or project

Confirm that the correct property or project is open before continuing the workflow.

Unexpected behaviour continues

If the problem remains after refreshing and checking the current workspace and project, record the exact action that caused the problem and any message displayed.

Tip A screenshot together with the exact action that caused the problem can make technical troubleshooting much faster.

Contact Support

Tell us what you need help with.

If you cannot find an answer in the Help Center, prepare a short description of the problem so that the issue can be investigated efficiently.

Before contacting support

  • Check the relevant Help Center article.
  • Note which workspace you were using.
  • Note the property or project involved.
  • Describe the exact step where the problem occurred.
  • Include any error message you saw.

What to include in a support request

A useful support request should explain what you were trying to do, what you expected to happen and what happened instead.

A screenshot can also be useful when the problem is visual or related to the platform interface.

Support The support request submission workflow will be connected here as the EMF Insight support system is implemented.
