Understanding Property Insights

Understand how EMF Insight presents your property's assessment results.

Property Insights brings together the information collected during your Home Wellness assessment and presents it in a way that helps you understand your property's environmental profile.

Property assessment

Your property assessment is built from the information, measurements and environmental data collected for the property.

The assessment becomes part of the property's ongoing Property Health Record.

How to read Property Insights

Property Insights brings together the available information from your assessment so that you can review the property as a whole and examine specific assessed areas.

The information shown depends on what was measured, recorded and assessed for the property.

Exposure results

Property Insights presents the available exposure information collected during the assessment, including the relevant measurement results and their location within the property.

Results by area

Where sufficient assessment data is available, results can be understood in relation to the rooms and areas where measurements were collected.

This helps connect individual measurement results with their location within the property.

Heatmaps and measurement coverage

Where measurements have been collected, visual results such as heatmaps help show how exposure varies across rooms and assessed areas.

Measurement coverage also provides context for understanding how much of the property has actually been assessed.

Why measurement coverage matters

Measurement coverage shows how much of the assessed property has actually been measured.

A result from a limited measurement area should therefore be understood within the context of the areas that were assessed and the areas that were not measured.

Sources and recommendations

Property Insights can also present identified EMF sources and recommendations related to the assessment.

Recommendations are intended to help you understand possible next steps based on the assessment information.

Full EMF Insight Report

When available, the complete EMF Insight Report provides a more detailed presentation of the property's assessment and findings.

Insights and recommendations

Property Insights can bring together assessment results, identified sources and recommendations in one view.

Recommendations are based on the information available from the assessment and are intended to help you identify possible next steps.

Important Property Insights reflect the information and measurements available for the property at the time of the assessment. Measurement coverage is therefore important context when interpreting results.

Home Wellness vs Business Survey

Understand the purpose of each EMF Insight workspace.

EMF Insight provides two separate workspaces designed for different assessment contexts: Home Wellness and Business Survey.

Home Wellness

Home Wellness is designed for individual property owners who want to understand the environmental profile of their home and its living areas.

A Home Property forms the foundation of the Home Wellness workflow and is connected to its Property Health Record.

Business Survey

Business Survey is designed for professional assessment workflows involving projects, floors, rooms, zones, measurements, heatmaps and reporting.

It provides a structured workspace for carrying out and documenting professional property assessments.

Choose the appropriate workspace

Use Home Wellness when you are working with your own property as a Home user.

Use Business Survey when carrying out professional assessment work and managing Business Survey projects.

Tip Your workspace determines which workflows, projects and account features are available to you.

Your Property Health Record

Understand the ongoing health record associated with your property.

Your Property Health Record is the ongoing record associated with your property. It provides the foundation for keeping information about the property's assessments connected over time.

One property, an ongoing record

A Home Property is not just a single assessment. Its Property Health Record provides a continuing place for the property's assessment information.

As new assessments and relevant information are added, the record can continue to develop with the property.

Property and Property Health Record

Each Home Property has its own Property Health Record. The record belongs to the property rather than to an individual assessment.

This means that assessment information can remain connected to the same property as the Home Wellness workflow develops.

Using your Property Health Record

Start by creating your Home Property. The property then becomes the starting point for your Home Wellness assessment.

As you continue working with the property, assessment results, insights and recommendations remain associated with that property.

This provides continuity when you return to the same property for further assessment or review.

Important

The Property Health Record is property-specific. Creating a new property creates a separate property context and does not merge its information with another property.

Assessments over time

As assessments are performed, they can be associated with the same property record. This allows the property's assessment history to develop over time.

Property information and assessments

The property profile provides the identity and context of the property, while assessments provide information collected during individual assessment activities.

Why this matters

Keeping assessment information connected to the property makes it possible to understand the property's environmental profile as an ongoing record rather than as isolated assessments.

Remember Your Property Health Record belongs to the property and can continue to develop as new assessments are added.

Property Insights

Understand the information and results available for your property.

Property Insights brings together the information available from your property assessment so you can understand what has been measured, where it was measured and what the assessment indicates.

Property overview

The Property Insights view provides an overview of the property's current assessment information and status.

Measurements and coverage

Assessment results can include measurement information and measurement coverage for the assessed areas of the property.

Coverage is important context because results reflect the areas that have actually been assessed.

Exposure information

Where measurement data is available, Property Insights can present the relevant exposure information collected during the assessment.

Sources and recommendations

The assessment may also identify relevant EMF sources and provide recommendations associated with the available assessment information.

Assessment context

Property Insights should be understood in the context of the assessment that produced the available data, including its measurement coverage and the areas assessed.

Important Property Insights are based on the information available from the property's assessment. They should be considered together with the assessment context and measurement coverage.

Full EMF Insight Report

Understand the complete report available for your property assessment.

The Full EMF Insight Report provides a more complete, structured presentation of the information generated during your property assessment.

When is the report available?

The Full EMF Insight Report becomes available when the required assessment information is present for the property and the report has been unlocked.

What does the report bring together?

The report brings together the available assessment information into a single document, making it easier to review and retain the results of the property assessment.

  • Property and assessment context
  • Assessment results
  • Coverage information
  • Relevant EMF sources
  • Recommendations
  • Supporting visual information

What is included in the report?

The Full EMF Insight Report brings the available assessment information together into a structured document that can be retained and reviewed outside the interactive dashboard.

  • Property and assessment context
  • Assessment results
  • Coverage information
  • Heatmap and other supporting visual information
  • Identified EMF sources, where available
  • Recommendations related to the assessment

Unlocking the report

The Full EMF Insight Report is a separate one-time purchase associated with the property assessment.

Once unlocked, the complete report can be generated for the property.

Report access

The Full EMF Insight Report is a separate report output from the interactive Property Insights view.

Where the report requires unlocking or purchase, the available option and current price are shown in the platform before you proceed.

Current pricing is displayed in the selected billing currency. See Billing & Plan for the current price and purchase options.

Report vs Property Insights

Property Insights provides the interactive assessment information within EMF Insight. The Full EMF Insight Report brings the available assessment information together into a complete document.

Interactive insights vs. report

Property Insights is designed for reviewing assessment information within EMF Insight.

The Full EMF Insight Report packages the available assessment information into a structured document that can be retained as a record of the assessment.

The two are therefore complementary: Insights support interactive review, while the report provides a consolidated document of the available assessment information.

Important The report reflects the assessment information available for the property at the time it is generated. If additional assessment information becomes available later, a new report may reflect the updated information.

Professional Assessment

Learn how to request professional help with your property assessment.

If you would like professional assistance with your property assessment, EMF Insight allows Home Wellness users to submit a professional assessment request.

Request a professional assessment

A professional assessment request allows you to indicate that you would like help from a qualified professional with your property and EMF assessment.

How the request works

A Professional Assessment request connects a Home Wellness property owner with professionals who are available to provide assessment services.

  1. Submit a Professional Assessment request for your property.
  2. Your request becomes available to eligible professionals.
  3. An interested professional can unlock your contact information.
  4. The professional can then contact you to discuss the assessment.

What happens after you submit a request?

Your request can become available to professionals who are eligible to respond to Home Wellness assessment requests.

Availability and response depend on the professionals participating in the EMF Insight professional network.

Professional contact access

Professionals use Contact Points to unlock contact information for available Professional Assessment requests.

Unlocking a request gives the professional access to the available contact information for a limited period.

14-day contact access

Once a professional unlocks your request, contact access remains available for 14 days.

After the 14-day period expires, the professional must unlock the request again if they need to regain contact access.

Contact Points

Each contact unlock uses one Contact Point from the professional's available allowance.

The Contact Point is used when contact access is unlocked. An expired 14-day access period does not automatically return the Contact Point to the professional's allowance.

If contact access is required again after expiry, another Contact Point is required.

Your property information

The professional assessment request is associated with your property and its available assessment information. This provides context for the professional reviewing the request.

Professional contact

When a professional takes an interest in an available request, the EMF Insight professional workflow controls how contact information becomes available.

What happens next?

After your request becomes available, professionals who are eligible to respond can review the request and decide whether to unlock contact access.

Availability and response depend on the professionals participating in the EMF Insight professional network.

EMF Insight does not guarantee that a professional will accept or respond to every request.

Important Submitting a request does not itself guarantee that a professional will accept or complete an assessment.

Creating a Business Project

Understand how a Business Survey project is organized.

A Business Project provides the workspace for carrying out and documenting a professional property survey.

Workspace

Business Survey is a separate workspace designed for professional assessment workflows.

Project

A project represents the property or survey engagement you are working on.

Project information provides the foundation for organizing the survey and its associated data.

One project, one property context

A Business Project keeps the information for a professional survey organized in one place.

Project information provides the context for the sessions, floors, rooms, zones and measurements created during the survey.

Session

A project can contain survey sessions. A session represents a particular assessment activity within the project.

Sessions allow survey work to be organized over time rather than treating every assessment as one continuous activity.

Why use sessions?

A session represents a specific survey activity within a project. This allows work on the same project to be organized into separate assessment activities.

For example, a project may contain an initial survey session and later sessions for additional assessment work.

Current Floor

Within a survey session, work is performed on the current floor of the property.

Floors provide the spatial structure for rooms, zones and measurements.

Working within a floor

The current floor is the spatial context in which you define rooms, zones and measurement areas.

When a property contains multiple floors, each floor can be handled as its own spatial level within the project.

Survey workflow

The Business Survey workflow guides you through the main stages of preparing and measuring the property.

  • Plan
  • Scale
  • Rooms
  • Zones
  • Measure

From plan to results

The workflow moves from preparing the property plan to defining the areas that will be assessed and then collecting measurements.

  1. Plan — prepare the floor plan.
  2. Scale — establish the drawing scale.
  3. Rooms — define the rooms.
  4. Zones — define relevant assessment zones.
  5. Measure — collect measurement data.

The resulting information can then be used for heatmaps, analysis and professional reporting.

Tip Keep your project structure organized from the beginning. It makes later measurements, results and reporting easier to manage.

Sessions, Floors & Rooms

Understand how the physical structure of a property is organized within a Business Survey.

Business Survey organizes assessment work around the physical structure of the property. Sessions, floors and rooms provide the structure in which survey data is collected and reviewed.

Sessions

A session represents a particular survey activity within a Business Project.

Sessions allow assessment work to be separated into distinct survey activities over time.

Session context

Each session belongs to a Business Project and provides the context for the survey activity being performed.

When you return to a project, the session helps keep the assessment work organized and connected to the property.

Floors

A property can contain multiple floors. The current floor determines the part of the property you are working on during the survey.

Each floor can contain its own rooms, zones and measurement information.

Working with multiple floors

If a property contains more than one floor, each floor can be organized separately within the project.

Rooms, zones and measurements belong to their relevant floor, helping keep results associated with the correct part of the property.

Rooms

Rooms define the individual spaces within a floor. They provide the spatial context for measurements, sources and assessment results.

Rooms as the measurement context

Rooms provide the spatial context for measurements and assessment information.

Organizing measurements by room makes it easier to understand where data was collected and how results relate to individual areas of the property.

Why the structure matters

Keeping sessions, floors and rooms organized makes it possible to understand where measurements were collected and how results relate to the property.

Recommended structure

For a clear professional survey, keep the physical structure consistent throughout the project:

  • Project
  • Session
  • Floor
  • Room
  • Zone
  • Measurement

This structure helps keep measurements, heatmaps, analysis and reports connected to the correct location.

Tip Complete the floor and room structure before collecting measurements so that your survey data remains spatially organized.

Zones & Measurements

Understand how survey areas and measurement data are organized.

Zones and measurements provide the structure for collecting and organizing environmental data during a Business Survey.

Zones

Zones define specific areas within the property that can be assessed separately from the overall room structure.

A zone can be used to organize measurements around a particular area or assessment context.

Zones within rooms

Zones provide a more specific assessment context within the property's room structure.

They can be used when a particular part of a room needs to be assessed or measured separately.

Measurement Profile

The Measurement Profile determines which measurement modalities are collected for the survey.

  • RF
  • Electric
  • Magnetic

The selected profile affects which measurements are collected and contributes to assessment completion and confidence.

Choosing a Measurement Profile

The Measurement Profile defines which measurement modalities are relevant for the survey area.

  • RF — radio frequency measurements
  • Electric — electric field measurements
  • Magnetic — magnetic field measurements

The selected profile determines which measurements are expected and contributes to assessment completion and confidence.

Measurement points

Measurement points represent locations where measurements are collected within the room or zone.

Multiple measurement points can be used to build a more complete picture of the assessed area.

Measurement grid and points

Measurement points define where readings are collected within the assessed room or zone.

Multiple points across an area allow the survey to capture spatial variation rather than relying on a single location.

The collected points provide the measurement data used for subsequent analysis and visualisation.

Coverage

Measurement coverage indicates how much of the relevant area has been measured.

Coverage provides important context when reviewing survey results and determining whether an area has been sufficiently assessed.

Measurement status

Measurement progress indicates whether the relevant area has been measured and whether the expected measurement work is complete.

  • Not measured — measurement work has not started.
  • Partial / In progress — some measurement data is available, but the area is not yet complete.
  • Confirmed — the relevant measurement work has been completed.

These statuses describe measurement progress and completion. They should not be interpreted as the property's risk level.

Important Measurement Profile determines what is collected. It does not itself determine the property's risk level.

Heatmaps & Results

Understand how measurement data is visualized and reviewed.

Heatmaps provide a visual representation of measurement data across the assessed areas of a property.

From measurements to a heatmap

Measurements collected at points within rooms and zones provide the underlying data used to generate the visual representation.

What a heatmap shows

A heatmap provides a visual representation of how measured values are distributed across the assessed area.

It helps you see spatial patterns in the measurement data and identify areas where measured values differ.

Spatial context

Because measurements are associated with locations within the property, the results can be reviewed in relation to rooms, zones and floors.

Reading results in context

Heatmap results should be viewed together with the room, zone and floor where the measurements were collected.

The spatial context helps you understand where the displayed measurement patterns occur within the property.

Measurement coverage

Coverage provides context for interpreting a heatmap. Areas without measurement data should not be interpreted as equivalent to areas that have been measured.

Heatmaps and coverage

A heatmap represents the measurement data available for the assessed area. It does not represent areas where no measurement data has been collected.

Measurement coverage should therefore be considered when reviewing and interpreting the visual result.

Reviewing results

Heatmaps can be reviewed together with the underlying measurements, sources and other assessment information available for the survey.

Different floors

Where a property contains multiple floors, each floor can have its own spatial assessment information and visual results.

Heatmap and assessment results

Heatmaps are one part of the Business Survey results. They work together with measurement data, room and zone information, sources and other assessment results.

The heatmap should therefore be understood as a visual representation of the available measurement data rather than as a standalone assessment conclusion.

Important A heatmap visualizes available measurement data. It does not replace the underlying measurements or their assessment context.

Business Survey Reports

Understand how survey information is brought together in reports.

Business Survey reports bring together the information collected during an assessment so that the results can be reviewed as a structured assessment.

What a report brings together

Report content can include information from the property structure, rooms, zones, measurements, sources, heatmaps and assessment results.

From survey to report

A Business Survey report is created from the information collected during the survey workflow. The quality and completeness of the report therefore depend on the information available in the project.

  1. Set up the property and floor structure.
  2. Define rooms and zones.
  3. Select the required Measurement Profile.
  4. Collect measurements at the required points.
  5. Review coverage, sources, heatmaps and assessment results.
  6. Generate the report from the completed assessment information.

Assessment context

Results should be interpreted together with the context of the survey, including the areas assessed and the measurement coverage available for those areas.

Reading a Business Survey report

The report should be read as a structured representation of the assessment rather than as a replacement for the underlying survey. Results are best understood together with the areas assessed, measurements collected and available measurement coverage.

Use the report to review the property as a whole and then examine individual floors, rooms, zones, measurements and identified sources where more detail is required.

Before generating a report

  • Check the property and floor structure.
  • Review rooms and zones.
  • Check available measurements.
  • Review measurement coverage.
  • Review sources and results.

Report and underlying survey data

A report is a presentation of the assessment information. The underlying survey data remains the basis for the results and should be retained as part of the assessment record.

Reports and assessment history

A generated report represents the assessment information available when the report is created. If the project is later updated with additional measurements or other assessment information, the updated project data remains the underlying source for subsequent analysis and reporting.

This allows the survey project to remain the working assessment environment while the report provides a structured presentation of the information for review or client communication.

Professional Branding

Professional Branding allows Business Survey reports to present your professional identity together with the assessment information.

Depending on your plan and branding access, this can include your company name, email, website and logo.

  • Single: Professional Branding is available as an optional add-on.
  • Pro: Professional Branding is available as an optional add-on.
  • Professional: Professional Branding is included in the plan.

When enabled, branding is used to support client-facing professional communication and branded reports.

Tip Review the survey results before generating the final report so that missing measurements or incomplete areas can be identified first.

Contact Points

Contact Points are used by professionals to unlock contact information from Home users who have requested a Professional Assessment.

How it works

  1. A Home user submits a Professional Assessment request.
  2. The request becomes available to eligible professionals.
  3. A professional can use one Contact Point to unlock the user's contact information.

Measurements & Results

Common checks when measurements or assessment results do not appear as expected.

Measurement results depend on the areas and points that have been measured. When reviewing results, first check the underlying survey structure and measurement coverage.

If measurements are not available

Check that a Measurement Profile has been selected and that the relevant measurement points have been completed.

The Measurement Profile determines which measurement modalities are collected, such as RF, Electric or Magnetic.

Coverage is incomplete

Incomplete coverage means that only part of the relevant area has measurement data.

Review the measurement points and complete the relevant areas before treating the results as a complete assessment.

Heatmap does not show the expected result

Check that the relevant area contains measurement data and that you are viewing the correct floor or room.

Remember that a heatmap represents available measurement data and should be interpreted together with coverage.

If a heatmap looks incomplete

Check the measurement coverage for the relevant room or zone. A heatmap can only represent measurement information that has been collected for the assessed area.

  • Check whether measurement points have been completed.
  • Check whether the relevant area has sufficient coverage.
  • Check that you are viewing the intended floor or area.

Results look different between areas

Different rooms or zones can contain different measurement coverage and measurement data.

Review the underlying measurements and spatial context before comparing results.

If measurement status is not confirmed

Review the measurement points for the relevant room or zone and complete any missing measurements.

Measurement status describes the state of measurement collection. It does not by itself determine the property's risk level.

Important Do not interpret an unmeasured area as equivalent to an area that contains confirmed measurement data.

Reports

Common checks when a report does not generate or does not contain the expected information.

Reports are generated from the assessment information available for the property or business survey.

Report does not generate

Check that the assessment contains the required survey information and that the relevant property, floor and measurement data are available.

If a report is missing information

Review the underlying assessment before generating the report. Check the property structure, rooms, zones, measurements, coverage, sources and assessment results.

If a heatmap or result is missing from the report

First check whether the relevant information exists in the underlying survey and assessment data.

A report presents the assessment information available for the project. It does not replace the underlying survey data.

If the report does not reflect recent changes

Review the current assessment data and generate the report again when the assessment information has been updated.

The generated report represents the assessment information available when the report was created.

Before contacting support

  • Check the property or project you are working with.
  • Check the relevant floor and room.
  • Check measurement coverage.
  • Check whether the underlying results are available.
Tip When reporting a report-generation problem, include the property or project, the report type and the step at which the problem occurred.
